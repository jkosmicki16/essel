What the EPA\'s New PFAS Rules Mean for Environmental Consultants

What Changed

On April 10, 2025, the EPA finalized the first-ever national drinking water standards for six PFAS compounds under the Safe Drinking Water Act. Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) were set at 4 parts per trillion (ppt) for PFOA and PFOS — the most common and well-studied of the so-called "forever chemicals."

This is a significant departure from previous advisory guidance, which was non-enforceable. Water systems now have five years to comply, but environmental consultants will feel the pressure much sooner as clients begin scoping assessment and remediation projects.

What It Means for Environmental Consultants

The rule creates three near-term opportunities for consulting firms:

  • Site assessment demand spike. Utilities and industrial clients will need rapid baseline sampling — particularly at sites near historic fire-training areas, military installations, and industrial wastewater discharges.
  • Remedial investigation scope expansion. Many existing RI/FS projects will need to be reopened to add PFAS characterization, which means contract amendments and additional field mobilizations.
  • Regulatory liaison work. Clients operating in multiple states face a patchwork of state rules that in some cases are stricter than the new federal MCLs. Navigating that matrix is billable work that didn\'t exist 18 months ago.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • MCLs of 4 ppt for PFOA/PFOS are now enforceable — not just advisory.
  • Five-year compliance window begins now; consulting work starts immediately.
  • 12 states have stricter state-level rules — know your jurisdiction.
  • Expect contract amendments on active RI/FS projects with PFAS exposure.
